The idea

MyFrontierAI is a personal AI operating environment: notes, projects, models, policies, and tools connected so that intelligence can work across context without making any single vendor, model, or platform the permanent center.

Sovereign by design. Frontier's governing policy requires a defined purpose, explicit authorization and an observable record for external processing. Implementation and enforcement are assessed separately from the written rule.

What “sovereign AI” means here. User-controlled computing, data governance, model selection, delegation policy, and technological autonomy. It does not refer to political sovereignty, legal immunity, or affiliation with the sovereign-citizen movement.

What Frontier is designed to support

Frontier is an evolving personal AI environment. Its Constitution defines the governing requirements; implementation proceeds through building, testing, observation and revision. A written policy alone is not evidence that a capability is operational or a control is enforced.

Governance

A written constitution and operating policy, applied before tools: human approval for consequential actions, independent review of important claims and decisions, and records of what acted, on whose authority, and with what result.

Second Brain (PARA + RAG)

Knowledge organized and made searchable by meaning, with explicit boundaries for what may be indexed, synchronized, or delegated.

Panel of Experts

Multiple local and external models used as interchangeable specialists, with disagreement and uncertainty preserved rather than flattened.

Personal AI Assistant

A human-directed assistant that can work from durable personal context while remaining constrained by policy, permissions, and oversight.

Sovereign Architecture

A zero-trust foundation combining local capability, network isolation, controlled delegation, backup, recovery, and auditability.

Prepared September 14, 2026 from retained project records; reviewed October 31, 2026. Next review by December 15, 2026. Each status describes the bounded capability at its evidence date. This is not a live health report or a guarantee of current availability.

Operational: observed in use. Built and tested: demonstrated within a stated test scope. Designed: documented approach with deployment unverified. Proposed: a next step awaiting a decision and implementation. Paused: built and once in use, now stopped, with the reason stated.

Per-reply claim checkerOperationalSince September 25, 2026: after every substantive reply from the main assistant, a separate reviewer run with a fresh context checks the reply's claims against the conversation and the tools' activity. Personal AI Assistant
Natural-voice readingOperationalOctober 7, 2026: an open-weight speech model (Kokoro-82M) installed from fingerprint-checked files reads pages and long replies aloud on this machine's processor, about 12 times faster than real time. October 8: no network connections seen during a monitored reading. October 7 to 31: in regular use for spoken copies of long replies, made on 19 of those 25 days. It now also voices scripted readings with several speakers, such as panel discussions.It reads text aloud; it does not converse. Some words are mispronounced. The network check sampled one run and was not a packet capture.
Overnight agent with enforced limitsPausedOctober 7 to 8, 2026: two permission tests on the production model refused every out-of-bounds action tried (reading outside its folders, writing outside its output folder, unlisted websites, shell commands), with no connectors loaded; one watched run and the first scheduled run completed with no denied actions. In all, four scheduled nights completed, October 8 to 11. October 12 and 13: the last two runs failed because the assistant's sign-in had expired; the October 13 run did not start. The one-week trial ended October 14.Stopped since October 13, and no review of the trial has been written yet. On native Windows its permission settings were the only guardrail, with no operating-system sandbox. It read private notes using a cloud model. It was not tested against prompt injection.
